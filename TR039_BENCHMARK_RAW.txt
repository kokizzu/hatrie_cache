Derived comparison
------------------
Feed append without subscriber / direct control: 4.72x CPU, 2 allocations
Feed append with one subscriber / direct control: 10.31x CPU, 4 allocations
Feed append with one subscriber / initial un-packed implementation: 0.70x CPU,
 0.54x bytes, and 0.33x allocations (275.8 ns/op versus 396.2 ns/op;
 160 B/op versus 296 B/op; 4 versus 12 allocs/op)

The direct channel is intentionally a weaker control. It does not retain
history, validate schema/sequence, detach caller bytes, support replay/ack,
or evict slow consumers. The feed is opt-in and should be used when those
correctness and recovery guarantees are required; ordinary paths are
unchanged.
